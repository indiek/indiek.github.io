Vision

The mission

To foster knowledge creation and knowledge management.

Human cognition can do far more in its interaction with the digital world than what exists today. Operating systems, apps, the web and AI still only scratch the surface of what a person can build when they think with a machine.

There is a big difference between having a ton of information online — as an encyclopedia does — and understanding, at a technical and individual level, a complex topic the way a molecular biologist understands their field. Access to information is not understanding. IndieK is built around that distinction.

IndieK aims at fostering knowledge creation at the level of individual minds, and collaborative knowledge generation through debate.

The vision

Two movements, one substrate:

  1. The individual. A person building genuine understanding of a complex topic needs to externalise structure, not just store documents. They need to connect, contradict, revise and re-read their own thinking over years — without their work being trapped in a vendor’s database or in a format that dies with the app that wrote it.
  2. The collective. Understanding sharpens against opposition. IndieK treats debate as a first-class, knowledge-generating activity rather than as noise, and models it on the same substrate as individual thought: a claim, a rebuttal and the edge between them are all signed, attributable and revisable.

Both rest on the same technical foundation: a durable, verifiable, implementation-independent knowledge graph archive that its authors own.

The bet

IndieK bets on knowledge graphs with very loose restrictions on the data format:

  • Versatile data may be attached to both nodes and edges. An edge is not a bare pointer: it carries content, and can be argued about, tagged and revised exactly like a node.
  • Tags are designed freely for both nodes and edges. IndieK ships no fixed ontology, no mandatory schema and no imposed taxonomy. You invent the vocabulary your topic needs.

This looseness is deliberate. A rigid schema encodes someone else’s understanding of your domain; premature structure is the enemy of knowledge creation. IndieK constrains durability — content-addressed data, a signed append-only history, archival-safe media types — and leaves meaning to you.

Built for the long run

An archive should outlive the software that wrote it, the company that published that software, and the cryptography it was signed with. That goal drives every technical choice:

Principle Consequence in the format
Your archive must outlive our software Plain folders and UTF-8 text logs; every archive carries its own specification, schemas and test vectors
The spec must be reimplementable One canonical JSON form (a subset of RFC 8785), SHA-256, Ed25519, an RFC 6962 Merkle tree — nothing exotic
History is knowledge, not clutter Append-only logs; nothing is edited or deleted, only superseded
Data belongs to nodes and edges Both carry content: managed in the archive, a URI, or a fingerprinted local file
Debate requires attribution Every event is signed by its author; only an item’s author may revise it
Tampering must be detectable Hash-chained events, content-addressed blobs, annotations anchored to the history they annotate
Checking must not depend on anyone Seals: one published hash lets anyone verify a copy offline
Cryptography ages Every hash and signature names its algorithm; transcription events re-attest old history under new algorithms
A flipped bit must not destroy an archive Line-oriented text: damage stays local and is always reported precisely

The details are in the format specification.

What exists today

Everything described on this site is free and open source:

  • The IndieK archive format, version 3.1 — the specification.
  • The indiek Python package, version 1.3.0: a Python SDK and the ik command-line tool, the reference implementation for creating, reading, verifying, sealing and exporting archives.
  • This website, with examples.
